How to Photograph Howe Sound From a Boat: Tips From the Water

November 18, 2026 | Squamish Water Taxi

Howe Sound is one of the best subjects in the Sea to Sky for landscape photography. Steep mountain walls, still water, drifting cloud and big skies all meet in one place. Shooting from a boat adds a few challenges, but with some preparation you can come home with images you can't get from the highway.

Chase the right light

  • Golden hour: the hour after sunrise and before sunset gives warm side-light on the mountains and glowing water.
  • Blue hour: the 20 to 30 minutes after sunset often produces soft pinks and purples, and the town lights start to appear on the shore.
  • Weather: don't write off cloudy days. Mist, low cloud and clearing storms are what make the best fjord pictures.

Camera settings for a moving boat

  • Shutter speed: 1/500 s or faster for handheld shots. The boat is always moving, even when it looks still.
  • Stabilization: switch on lens or in-body stabilization, then tuck your elbows in and use your body as a tripod.
  • Aperture and ISO: f/5.6 to f/8 gives sharp scenes. Raise the ISO as the light drops rather than slowing the shutter.
  • Focus: continuous autofocus helps when the boat is rocking. For distant mountains, a single focus point on the ridge works well.
  • Shoot RAW: you'll want the extra room to recover highlights in the sky.

What to bring (and what to leave)

  • A zoom that covers wide to short telephoto, such as 24-105 mm or similar. Changing lenses on a boat is a good way to get spray on your sensor.
  • A lens cloth, and a dry bag or hard case for the camera when you're moving.
  • A circular polarizer to cut glare on the water and deepen the sky.
  • A neck or wrist strap you actually trust.
  • Leave the tripod at home. It won't work on a moving boat.

Scenes worth looking for

  • Island silhouettes: layered ridgelines of Anvil and the other islands against a pastel sky.
  • Mirror reflections: early mornings and evenings are often calm enough to double a peak in the water.
  • Waterfalls and sea walls: the cliffs along the Sound drop straight into the water, and waterfalls pour into the fjord after rain.
  • Wildlife: seals, eagles and, with luck, larger marine mammals. Keep a respectful distance. Our wildlife guide has more.
  • The Stawamus Chief: see our guide to Squamish from the water.

Talk to your captain

Tell us at the start what you hope to shoot. A good skipper can time a stop for the best light, position the boat so the sun is behind you and keep the engines quiet while you work. See the tours on our tours page, or get in touch for a private charter built around your shoot.

Frequently Asked Questions

What camera settings work best on a moving boat?

Use a fast shutter speed, around 1/500 s or quicker for sharp images in motion, and turn on image stabilization if your lens has it. Let ISO rise as the light fades rather than slowing the shutter.

Can I fly a drone from the boat?

Drone rules in Canada are set by Transport Canada and you must hold the right certificate for most flights. Launching and landing from a moving boat is risky, so ask us before the trip and never fly near wildlife.

Will you stop the boat for photos?

Yes, we're happy to slow down or hold position for the scenes you want, as long as conditions and safety allow. Let your captain know your plan at the start of the trip.
